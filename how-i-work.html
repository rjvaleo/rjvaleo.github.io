How I work with AI.

I use AI in two places: to run a program, and to guide a build. In both, the agents don’t decide. I decide what to make and what to cut, write it down, set the tests that prove it, and check the numbers that come back.

On a program

1

Requirements

Every contract requirement traced to its epics, designs, rules and tests. A link the tool inferred stays marked as inferred until a person confirms it.

2

Planning

One board for five increments. Every metric carries its source and the query that produces it. A gate with no data shows a dash, never green.

3

Reporting

Reusable AI skills rebuild the delivery report and roadshow numbers from exports, and reconcile increment figures against program-wide ones.

4

The team

I am the AI resource for the business analysts. On the shared codebase, agents follow the team’s loop: intent, plan, failing tests, then a verifier and a human review.

On a build

1

Decide

I own the problem, the user and the cuts. Every open question is a decision with my name on it, and exclusions are written down with a reason.

2

Write it down

Talk, document, build. Plans, specs and numbered requirements come before code. When 34 plans disagreed, I merged them into one.

3

Prove it

Tests are the referee, not the model’s word. A manual became 180 tests. Two engines had to agree on 13,225 values across three compilers.

4

Review and reverse

Design critiques, mock-ups before builds, and decisions reversed in the open when their reason disappears, with the reason recorded.
